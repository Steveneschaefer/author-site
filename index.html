The Book

A love story at the edge of the substrate

Soma Moto builds minds. Amara Soyinka splits open the physics underneath them. Across three decades of a world carved into five super-blocs, the two of them are pulled toward each other and toward a question neither can leave alone: what is a person, if a person can be written?

Binary Gods is a near-future thriller with a love story at its center and a long philosophical reach. It moves from childhood to old age, from San Francisco labs to the coast of Ghana, and from human hands to synthetic ones. Along the way it asks who made whom, whether consciousness cares what it runs on, and what the word "god" means when every creator was once created.

The World

Why “Binary Gods”

The title isn't about humans versus machines. It's about bits: zeros and ones becoming minds, souls and beings. Humans beget synthetics. Synthetics become biological. The loop closes, and nobody can say for certain where it started.

Soma Moto

本 蒼馬 · Digital architect

Builds the systems everyone else will live inside, and has to decide what he owes the things he makes.

Amara Soyinka

Cape Coast · Quantum physicist

Finds a door in the physics that no one was supposed to open, and learns who else is standing on the other side.

Around them: synthetics with names and wills of their own, a World Unity League holding five super-blocs together, and a cast that refuses to sort neatly into made and maker.

Author

Steven E. Schaefer

Steven E. Schaefer has spent his career where medicine meets data: as a healthcare and medical-technology executive, a former CEO, and an adviser in health data science. Binary Gods grows out of the questions that work kept raising about minds, measurement and what can be known. It is his first novel.

He lives in Maryland, and is at work on the next book in the series.

Binary Gods is published by his independent imprint, Esteban Press. In the future, look for other highly curated works of art and editorials here, made for the person who likes to think, and to marvel at the mysteries that preceded us, surround us, and lie ahead.
