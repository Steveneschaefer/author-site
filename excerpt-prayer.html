Binary Prayer

A balmy breeze gently swept his brow. It bore a thin sweetness like jasmine and moved through the trees with a silence of its own. The ambient sounds of nature's symphony drifted into him. First the full orchestra of the wild, then he focused on its smaller parts, distinct sounds, attributing each to its source. The song of a Chinese rubythroat. The whisper of the wind in the reeds. The faint lament of a shakuhachi carried from some forgotten distance. He was aware and intentional. Time passed without weight or measurement.

Perched in a lotus position on the cliffs at Drak Yerpa, where Mumun had sent him, he attended to the fleeting occupations of his mind. He sought to greet each thought without judgment. He imagined painting them as clouds, not to capture them, only to let them float away. To be a blank page, void of all but potential. When his mind cleared, he would be free to dive deeper into meditation, to probe the edges of enlightenment. He would sit for hours. His brush posed above the paper.

I am waiting for the moment to begin. The paper is not empty. It is full of possibility. Let it speak. Let the ink settle, like silt in water.

Hours passed and the mind turned of its own accord. He let thinking return to him and pondered the old questions. Contemplation of the contrast between dharma-nature and Buddha-nature. Reality as it is and the self that peers upon it.

He was searching, not for answers, but for the edge where reality ends and self begins. For the past two years, The Song of Enlightenment had shaped his Zen practice, resonating through him until study and being felt inseparable. Seongcheol was only twenty-six years old, already on his path with the wisdom of an ancient soul.

Prayer for him was not supplication but a reaching toward the unknown. He sought communion with the hidden thing beneath all living things—that which he did not yet fully understand. He felt pulled toward something he could only describe as the common denominator of all that is, was, or will be. The smallest was the greatest. So he trained his mind to process his mental petitions into a binary form of primordial oppositions.

He strove to process the most complex of his meditations into a deconstructed form consisting of two basic elements: 0 or 1, on or off, positive or negative. A breath in and out.

He imagined each breath as a bit of truth, each moment of awareness as a stored value.

His meditation became strings of consciousness, an algorithm for grace.

He reached through the veil of matter toward the foundation beneath it.

He connected to the elemental pattern comprising everything.

Like a binary prayer.
