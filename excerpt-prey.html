Prey

She woke in the back of a customized, rust-flecked hauler van, unsure how long she'd been out. The windows were covered with scavenged carbon mesh. The air reeked of humidified body odor mixed with cigarette smoke, curry and drugstore cologne.

Attempting to move sent pain through her stiff back.

Amara recognized she'd been kidnapped. She knew the usual outcome for a Charm City abduction: ransom attempt, panicked response, and then a corpse left somewhere.

Why did she take the shortcut? She let herself feel rushed because she was impatient. Why did she take that risk? She allowed herself to be easy prey.

The odds favored this being her last day on Earth.

Amara was unprepared for suffering, but even less ready to face nonexistence. As she pondered her mortality, lines rose unbidden, like a hand from a dark pool:

Entropy will claim

Your body in parts

Nothing will remain

But love in our hearts

Buried in the earth, stillness would take her until decay returned her energy. Cremated, the heat and light of her would scatter through the galaxy, indistinguishable from all other wandering energy. Everything gathered throughout her brief lifetime, released.

To her parents, Amara's life and death would be nothing more than a memory.

Nausea rose hard; bile filled her throat. Panic surged, then dulled into something worse: apathy. Amara yearned to dispel the weakness, to recover her resolve, to gather her mind toward a practical purpose.

Those attempts were cut short.
